## Why the real spread is still this wide

- **Movement type sets a baseline.** Quartz Tank Must models are the most accessible; mechanical Tank Solo and higher references command a real premium for the movement alone.
- **Case material moves price dramatically.** Steel, vermeil (gold-plated silver), and solid precious-metal Tanks are different products even within the same silhouette.
- **Vintage and rare dial variants** attract collector demand that has little to do with the watch's basic specification, similar to how rare colorways affect handbag pricing.

## How to vet a cheap Tank listing

- **Confirm the model first.** Check that the case shape, dial layout, and any visible screws match the Tank's rectangular silhouette rather than a different Cartier family like the Santos.
- **Confirm it's the full watch head,** not just a strap or bracelet listed with overlapping keywords.
- **Sapphire cabochon crown.** Should be a correctly colored blue gemstone, cleanly set; a plastic-looking or off-color cabochon is a common fake tell.
- **Roman numeral dial print,** which should be crisp and evenly weighted with no blurring.
- **Case back serial number,** checkable against era-appropriate formatting, and get independent authentication before paying above a few thousand dollars.

## Buying to resell at this price point

The quartz Tank Must trades with the most consistent liquidity and is the safer entry point for a first purchase. Precious-metal and vintage references hold wider margin potential but carry more model-confusion risk in search results, so verify carefully before comparing a "deal" price to the ranges above.
