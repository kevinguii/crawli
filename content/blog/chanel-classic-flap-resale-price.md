## Why the same bag costs so much more on one site

- **Authentication changes what "Classic Flap" means.** StockX and Vestiaire authenticate before listing, so their prices reflect verified condition and rarity. A peer-to-peer listing at a fraction of that price is usually a different bag: more wear, no box, no card, sometimes a different (cheaper) leather or size sold under the same generic title.
- **Leather and hardware finish move price more than platform does.** Black caviar leather with gold hardware is the most common and most liquid combination. Rare exotic skins, all-white lambskin, or discontinued hardware finishes (like the older CC turnlock) push the same silhouette meaningfully higher regardless of where it's listed.
- **Box, card, and dust bag completeness.** A bag with its original packaging and authenticity documentation sells for more everywhere, but the gap matters most on authenticated platforms, which weight documentation heavily in their own condition grading.

## How to vet a cheap Classic Flap listing

- **Authenticity card and serial format.** Chanel moved to a microchip authentication system in 2021, replacing the older serial sticker. A "recent" bag with an old-style sticker, or a serial number that doesn't match the claimed production year, is a clear red flag.
- **CC turnlock weight and symmetry.** Genuine hardware has real heft and perfectly aligned lettering. Light, hollow-feeling hardware or slightly uneven lettering is common on fakes.
- **Quilting consistency.** Diamond stitching should be even in size and spacing across the whole bag, including the back panel, which counterfeiters sometimes rush.
- **Interior leather and smell.** Genuine lambskin and caviar have a distinct, non-chemical smell. A strong plastic or glue odor is a warning sign.
- **Request close-up photos** of the turnlock, interior stamp, and authenticity card before paying on any listing priced well under the rest of the market.
