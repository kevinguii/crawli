## Why the real spread is still this wide

- **Gold color and diamond setting are the biggest legitimate price drivers.** Plain yellow, white, and rose gold versions price similarly to each other, while diamond-set versions carry a real premium tied to stone count and quality.
- **Size affects gold content directly,** since a larger bracelet simply contains more metal, which shows up proportionally in price more than it would on a leather item.
- **Documentation and screw-system condition matter to buyers,** since the bracelet's iconic screw closure is a functional mechanism that can wear or be replaced with non-original screws over years of use.

## How to verify a Love bracelet before you pay

- **Interior hallmark stamps.** Should show a gold purity mark (750 for 18k), a serial number, and Cartier's maker's mark, all cleanly and precisely stamped.
- **Screw system function.** Genuine screws should thread smoothly and match the bracelet's era-appropriate design; loose, mismatched, or non-functioning screws are a red flag.
- **Weight and density.** Solid 18k gold has a specific, substantial heft; a bracelet that feels unusually light for its size may be hollow or plated rather than solid gold.
- **Serial number cross-check,** where possible, against Cartier's documented format for the piece's claimed era.
- **Get independent authentication and, ideally, a gold purity test** before paying, especially on any private listing without a paper trail.

## Buying to resell at this price point

Because the gold content itself sets a hard price floor, there's less room here for a dramatically underpriced genuine find than on a handbag or even a watch. When a real deal does surface, it's usually a private seller unaware of current gold prices rather than a market inefficiency.
