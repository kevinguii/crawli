**Short answer:** For most vintage Cartier jewelry, the best platform is the one that nets you the most after fees, not the one with the highest list price. eBay offers the widest reach and a roughly 13% fee, which suits iconic, liquid pieces like a Love bracelet or Juste un Clou. Curated and consignment platforms (Vestiaire Collective, The RealReal, 1stDibs) add authentication that reassures buyers and can lift the sale price on rarer pieces, but their higher commissions can quietly erase that premium. The right answer depends on the exact piece, so run the payout math before you list.

A vintage Cartier Tank Francaise bracelet that sells for $2,100 on eBay and $3,200 on a curated platform looks like an easy decision until you subtract fees. After a roughly 13% eBay fee the seller keeps around $1,825; after a 20%-plus curated commission the $3,200 sale can net closer to $2,500. The gap is real, but it is smaller than the sticker prices suggest, and on lower-priced pieces the ranking can flip entirely.

## Why Fees Beat List Price

Every platform advertises the buyer-facing number. What lands in your account is that number minus a commission that ranges from roughly 13% to more than 20% depending on where you sell. On a four-figure Cartier piece, a ten-point difference in commission is worth hundreds of dollars, which is often more than the price premium a fancier platform earns you in the first place. So the decision is not "where does this list highest," it is "where do I keep the most," and those are frequently different platforms.

Fee structures shift regularly on all of these, and consignment platforms in particular use sliding scales, so confirm the current rate for your specific price tier before you list rather than trusting a number you read months ago.

## When Auction Urgency Wins, and When It Doesn't

eBay's format works in your favor for the most iconic, liquid pieces. A Love bracelet or a Juste un Clou is recognizable enough that a broad audience competes for it, and auction urgency can push the final number past a fixed-price listing. That same format works against obscure vintage: a piece that only three collectors in the world want will not find its bidder in a seven-day window, and it sells for less than it would sitting patiently on a curated platform where the right buyer eventually walks past it.

The parallel here is watches. If your piece is actually a Cartier Tank rather than jewelry, the platform calculus shifts toward watch-specific marketplaces, which is its own decision covered in [the best place to sell a vintage Cartier Tank](/blog/best-place-to-sell-vintage-cartier-tank).

## Provenance Closes the Sale on Every Platform

Across all five options, the original box, papers, and any Cartier receipt or service record have an outsized effect on both speed and final price. On curated platforms especially, buyers are paying partly for confidence, and a complete original set plus the precise reference name (Love, Juste un Clou, Trinity, Tank) does most of the trust-building before a buyer has to ask a single question. A generic "vintage Cartier bracelet" listing with no documentation leaves money on the table no matter where you post it.
