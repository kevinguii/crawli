If you've ever bought secondhand fashion online, you already know the frustration: the exact same piece can be **$80 on one platform and $200 on another**. The seller, the condition, even the photos might be identical — but the price isn't.

Checking eight apps by hand is exhausting. Here's a faster way to think about it.

## Why prices vary so much

A few things drive the spread:

- **Audience.** Grailed skews streetwear and designer menswear; Poshmark leans contemporary and women's; StockX is sneakers and hype.
- **Seller fees.** Each marketplace takes a different cut, and sellers price accordingly.
- **Liquidity.** A piece that's rare on one platform might be common on another.

![Comparing the same item across marketplaces](/blog/images/best-resale-deals-cover.svg)

## A simple checklist before you buy

1. **Search the exact model name**, not just the brand. "Raf Simons bomber" returns noise; the specific season or style code does not.
2. **Compare total cost**, including shipping and any buyer protection fees.
3. **Check sold listings** where you can — they tell you what the market actually pays.
4. **Don't anchor on the first result.** The cheapest listing is rarely on the first platform you check.

> The goal isn't to find *a* price. It's to find the *distribution* of prices, then buy near the bottom of it.
