**Short answer:** eBay hosts far more listings than any watch-specific marketplace, but that volume works against sellers of genuinely desirable vintage pieces as often as it works for them. Curated marketplaces like Chrono24 and 1stDibs attract a smaller, more targeted audience of collectors who arrive already prepared to pay close to full asking price for the right piece, which often means a vintage Cartier Tank sells faster there than it would sitting in the flood of listings on a general marketplace, even accounting for differences in seller fees.

High-net-worth watch buyers are not scrolling eBay hoping to stumble onto a bargain the way a casual thrift shopper browses Poshmark. They are searching specific, curated platforms where the listings around them have already been filtered for quality and legitimacy, and they are prepared to pay accordingly.

## What Makes the Cartier Tank a Collector's Watch

The Tank has been in continuous, recognizable production since 1917, with enough design variation across decades, Tank Louis, Tank Francaise, Tank Must, Tank Americaine, and more, that buyers searching for a vintage piece typically know exactly which variant, case metal, and era they want before they start looking. That specificity is precisely the kind of demand that a curated, search-driven marketplace serves well, and that a general marketplace, where the same search returns everything from quartz reissues to unrelated brands, tends to serve poorly.

## Buyer Mentality: High-Traffic vs. Curated Platforms

A buyer arriving at a listing through eBay's search has typically already filtered through a huge volume of unrelated or lower-quality results to get there, and often arrives price-anchored to the cheapest comparable listing they passed along the way. A buyer arriving at the same watch through Chrono24 or 1stDibs has typically already accepted that they're shopping in a curated, trust-signaled environment, and is comparing your listing against other similarly vetted pieces rather than against the entire unfiltered used-watch market.

That difference in buyer mindset is the core reason curated platforms can produce a faster sale and a stronger final price for a genuinely collectible piece, even when the platform's fee structure is less favorable than a general marketplace's.

## Why Provenance and Original Accessories Close the Sale

Across every platform, the presence of the original box, papers, and a Cartier-signed strap or bracelet has an outsized effect on both sell-through speed and final price for a vintage Tank. Buyers on curated platforms in particular are paying partly for confidence that what they're buying is exactly what it's described as, and a complete original set, combined with a precise, correct model name rather than a generic "vintage Cartier watch" description, does most of the work of establishing that confidence before a buyer ever has to ask a question.

## Weighing Fees Against Speed and Price

The lowest seller fee isn't automatically the best choice if it comes attached to a buyer base less prepared to pay full value for a genuinely collectible piece. A watch that sells quickly at a strong price on a 6.5%-commission platform nets more than one that lingers for months on a lower-fee general marketplace before eventually selling below its real value. Matching the platform to the specific piece, common versus rare, documented versus undocumented, matters more than optimizing for the lowest fee percentage in isolation.
