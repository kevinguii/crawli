A listing that specifically mentions the original Certificate of Authenticity card tends to sell for more than an otherwise identical bag without one, since the card is one of the clearest ways to confirm a specific production run on a nylon bag.

## Why the same bag costs so much more on one site

- **Silhouette matters more than most Prada buyers expect.** The 2000 mini bag and 2005 shoulder bag are different products with different demand, even though both fall under "Re-Edition nylon."
- **Documentation carries real weight on this specific model.** Because the nylon material offers fewer visual cues than leather, the Certificate of Authenticity card is a bigger factor in buyer confidence and price than on most luxury bags.
- **Hardware finish and color affect demand unevenly.** Certain color and hardware combinations see more consistent buyer interest, which shows up as a price gap even between two bags in equally good condition.

## How to vet a cheap Re-Edition listing

- **Prada triangular logo plate.** Should be substantial metal with crisp, evenly spaced, deeply stamped lettering.
- **Nylon texture.** Genuine Re-Nylon has a specific matte, slightly ribbed feel; a shinier or looser-weave nylon is a warning sign.
- **Zipper pulls.** Should be branded, smooth-running, and proportioned correctly to the bag size.
- **Certificate of Authenticity.** Ask whether the card is included; its absence doesn't confirm a fake, but its presence is a meaningful positive signal.
- **Request close-up photos of the logo plate and zipper hardware** before paying on any listing priced well under the rest of the market.
