## Why the same "wallet" covers such a wide range

- **Wallet type is the single biggest price driver.** Cardholders, bifolds, and zippy wallets with multiple compartments are different products even in identical canvas and condition.
- **Canvas versus leather changes the price tier.** Standard coated monogram canvas and rarer full-leather or limited-edition print versions command very different prices.
- **Completeness affects small leather goods more than bags,** since a wallet missing its dust bag or box is a bigger proportional loss in perceived value than the same gap on a larger item.

## How to vet a cheap wallet listing

- **Heat-stamped date code.** One of the most reliable checks available on small leather goods; verify the format and factory location against known reference charts.
- **Canvas print registration.** Should be crisp and consistently aligned at every seam.
- **Zipper hardware,** on zippy styles, which should be a recognizable branded pull with smooth, consistent action.
- **Interior card slot stitching,** which should be even and consistently spaced throughout.
- **Confirm the exact wallet type** before comparing a listing's price to others.

## Turning a well-sourced wallet into margin

Source common cardholders and bifolds near the Mercari or Depop floor, and resell toward the Vestiaire or StockX range. Wallets turn over faster than bags but carry thinner per-item margin, so volume matters more here. Run the numbers with the [Crawli margin calculator](/) before committing.
