That gap between Depop's $9 floor and StockX's $320 floor isn't a 35x markup on the same jeans. It's two different products: an ordinary worn pair versus a verified rare vintage or deadstock pair that StockX's collector audience is specifically hunting.

## Why the two markets look nothing alike

- **StockX skews toward collector-grade vintage.** Its listings are pre-1990s production runs, deadstock with tags, or specific documented eras, not the everyday pair someone donated after wearing it for years.
- **Peer-to-peer platforms carry the everyday supply.** Depop, Vinted, Poshmark, and Mercari are where most ordinary 501s in common washes and eras actually sit, priced for someone who wants to wear them.
- **Fit is measured, not tagged, on both ends.** Whichever market you're shopping, the tag size on a vintage 501 rarely matches the actual measured waist after decades of shrinkage. [Our guide on measuring vintage 501s](/blog/how-to-buy-vintage-levis-501s-online) covers exactly how to request and read a seller's measurement before buying, on any platform, at any price point.

## How to tell if a cheap pair is actually rare

- **Care tag format.** The 501-0845 tag (1971 to early 1980s) and earlier no-tag Big E era pairs are the ones command real collector value.
- **Red tab font.** Capital "E" (Big E, pre-1971) versus lowercase "e" narrows the era immediately.
- **Back button stamp.** A "555" stamp indicates Valencia Street, San Francisco manufacture, particularly valued by collectors, per the details in our [vintage Levi's sizing guide](/blog/how-to-buy-vintage-levis-501s-online).
- **Condition and completeness.** Original buttons and an unreplaced zipper matter as much as era for collector value.
