Before treating a low number as a great deal, check whether the listing is actually the MoonSwatch (plastic case, quartz movement, co-branded Omega x Swatch) rather than the steel mechanical Speedmaster Professional. Both are genuine products, but they're not comparable watches, and the MoonSwatch accounts for most sub-$500 "Speedmaster" listings on peer-to-peer platforms.

## Why the real spread is still this wide

- **The MoonSwatch and the steel Professional are different products entirely,** despite sharing the Speedmaster name and general design language, which explains most of the apparent bargain listings.
- **Vintage pre-Moon references** (the earliest Speedmaster models predating the watch's association with NASA's Moon missions) command significant collector premiums over modern production.
- **Full sets with original box, papers, and service history** sell for meaningfully more than a watch-only listing, and curated platforms weight this heavily.

## How to vet a cheap Speedmaster listing

- **Confirm it's not a MoonSwatch first.** Check the case material (plastic and ceramic versus steel) and movement type (quartz versus mechanical) against what the listing claims.
- **Caseback engraving.** Professional models typically carry mission-specific text; should be crisp and deeply engraved, never shallow or blurry.
- **Chronograph pusher action,** which should be precise and tactile, not loose or mushy.
- **Bracelet end links,** which should fit flush against the case with no visible gap on a genuine watch.
- **Get independent third-party authentication** before paying on any vintage reference, where component swaps over decades make verification more complex than on modern production.

## Buying to resell at this price point

The modern steel Professional is the most liquid entry point and trades predictably across platforms. Vintage references hold wider margin but require real authentication expertise given how often components get mismatched over a watch's decades of ownership.
