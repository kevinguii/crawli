## Post 2 — Selvedge Denim Facts (Vintage Community)

**TikTok Title**
```
The denim detail most secondhand shoppers walk right past
```

**TikTok Description**
```
Selvedge denim is everywhere in vintage and raw denim listings, but most shoppers do not actually know what they are looking at or why it matters. Here is the breakdown.

Selvedge denim comes from old shuttle looms that weave a narrower bolt of fabric with a tightly bound edge that cannot fray. That clean self finished edge is the entire definition of the term.

The weaving process is the reason it holds up. Shuttle looms run narrower and slower than modern looms, which produces a denser, tighter weave than mass market denim ever gets.

The colored line along the seam, often red, simply marks which mill or era a piece of fabric came from. A different color does not automatically mean lower quality, it is just a different maker's signature.

That tighter weave is also why selvedge denim is considered higher quality. It fades and creases exactly where you move, building a pattern that mass produced denim never develops on its own.

Spotting it while thrifting is simple once you know what to check. Fold the cuff back and look for a clean woven edge instead of a serged one. A thin colored line inside that fold means you found the real thing.
```

## Post 5 — Handmade vs Machine Made (Fashionistas)

**TikTok Title**
```
How to tell handmade clothing from machine made
```

**TikTok Description**
```
Handmade and machine made garments can look nearly identical in a listing photo, but a few construction details give it away every time once you know what to check.

What actually counts as handmade: hand finished seams, irregular stitch spacing, and small construction quirks are the real signal, not a tag that just says crafted.

What machine made construction looks like: perfectly even stitch length and identical spacing on every seam usually means an industrial machine made the piece, not a person.

The detail that proves it: no two handmade garments are ever perfectly identical. Tiny irregularities are the actual proof, not a flaw to avoid.

Why handmade pieces cost more: time is the entire cost difference. A hand finished hem alone can take ten times longer than a machine hem.

Where quality actually comes from: a well made machine piece can outlast a sloppy handmade one. Construction quality matters more than which method made it.
```

## Post 6 — Wool Quality Facts (Fashionistas)

**TikTok Title**
```
The number on a wool tag that actually tells you the quality
```

**TikTok Description**
```
Not all wool is the same, and the difference comes down to a number most shoppers walk right past on the tag.

What actually defines wool quality: fiber fineness, measured in microns, is the real quality marker, not the brand name on the label.

The number to know: 19.5 microns or finer is generally considered premium merino wool. Thicker fibers feel rougher and pill faster over time.

Sheep breeds that produce the finest wool: merino sheep are bred specifically for fine, soft fiber, which is why merino shows up constantly on quality wool garments.

How to check wool grade while shopping: feel the fabric against your wrist. Premium wool feels soft immediately, and coarse wool announces itself just as fast.

Why investing in good wool pays off: quality wool resists wrinkles, regulates temperature, and holds its shape across years of wear in a way cheaper blends cannot match.
```
