## Post 01 — The Fee Math Before You List
`topic_key: platform-fee-netting-comparison`
`#platform-fees #reseller-tips`

**TikTok Hook (first line):**
The same $500 bag can net you $37 more or less, and the fee percentage on the listing page does not tell you that.

**Caption:**
A designer bag priced at $500 does not net the same amount everywhere, and the gap comes from more than the headline commission rate.

Poshmark takes 20% on any sale $15 or up, which nets $400 here. That fee bundles in a prepaid USPS label, so there is no separate shipping charge to negotiate around with a buyer.

eBay runs roughly a 13.6% final value fee plus a $0.40 per-order fee, netting closer to $432. Handbags $500 and up also get free Authenticity Guarantee coverage baked in, no extra charge. The tradeoff is that luxury search traffic on eBay is far less consistent than the dedicated resale platforms.

Vestiaire Collective's commission is tiered by price. A $500 item lands in the 18% bracket, netting $410 before you account for the roughly $12 to $18 you cover to ship it to their authentication hub, which lands you closer to $395.

The RealReal does not publish a flat rate at all. Payout scales with price tier and consignor history, so the exact same bag can net two different sellers two different amounts. It tends to pull serious, higher-intent buyers, but the number is the least predictable of the four before you list.

Run the actual math, not the headline percentage, before you decide where to list. Shipping costs, per-order fees, and authentication-hub charges all live outside that number on the listing page.

## Post 02 — What the Badge Actually Covers
`topic_key: authenticity-guarantee-scope-by-platform`
`#authenticity-check #luxury-resale`

**TikTok Hook (first line):**
Five platforms, five different definitions of "authenticated." None of them mean the same thing.

**Caption:**
An authentication badge feels like a single universal promise. It is not. Each platform draws its own line for what gets checked.

Poshmark's Posh Authenticate kicks in at $500 and covers a defined brand list, Chanel, Louis Vuitton, Gucci, and Rolex among them, plus handbags, watches, jewelry, and select sneakers. It is free for both buyer and seller, but only inside that list.

eBay's Authenticity Guarantee is gated by both category and price: handbags at $500 or more (or a $40 add-on service from $200), watches at $2,000 or more (or an $80 add-on from $500), and select sneaker models. Anything outside those categories or under the threshold gets no check.

Vestiaire Collective runs every listing through digital screening plus physical review from an in-house team of 80-plus authenticators, each with 750 hours of onboarding training. That is real depth, but it is Vestiaire evaluating its own inventory, not an independent third-party lab.

The RealReal's authentication is handled internally too, and the process has been publicly tested: the company paid an $11 million investor settlement over how rigorously that process was actually followed, and Chanel separately sued alleging counterfeits reached the site.

Grailed's "Digital Authentication" badge is AI image-matching combined with human review of the photos a seller submits, tags, stitching, hardware. It is a meaningful check, but it is not the hands-on physical inspection Vestiaire or Poshmark's process involves.

None of this means the badges are worthless. It means knowing what each one actually covers, price floor, brand list, physical versus photo review, before you treat it as your only line of defense.
