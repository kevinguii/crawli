```

**TikTok Title**
```
6 habits that stop you from getting burned buying secondhand
```

**TikTok Description**
```
The buyers who never get burned on secondhand are not luckier. They just run a few checks every time, before they pay, not after.

Habit 1: Check the seller first. Look at their rating and how long they have been selling before the item itself. A thin history or a string of recent complaints is worth pausing on, no matter how good the photos look.
Habit 2: Ask before you buy, not after. Request extra photos or exact measurements while you still can. Once you have paid, a seller has a lot less reason to answer quickly.
Habit 3: Read the return policy. Plenty of resale apps sell everything as final. Know that going in instead of finding out the hard way when the fit is wrong.
Habit 4: Compare condition, not just price. Two listings at the same price can describe very different wear. The cheaper one is not a deal if it is also the more damaged one.
Habit 5: Sleep on the big ones. Give yourself a day on anything over your normal budget. Almost every regret purchase was decided in the first five minutes of seeing it.
Habit 6: Let alerts do the waiting. Save a search for the specific grail instead of refreshing five apps by hand.

A few seconds of checking saves you from most bad purchases.

Crawli surfaces listings from across the major resale platforms side by side, so comparing condition and price before you commit takes one search, not five. thecrawli.com

#secondhandshopping #buyerguide #resaletips #thrifted #smartshopping #buyersguide #thriftfinds

Knowing a real vintage tee from a reprint is only half the job. Knowing where to actually find one is the other half, and most of it happens before you ever check a tag.

Spot 1: Hit local thrift early. Go right after open, before the day pickers clear the good racks. Check the oversized and men's sections regardless of your size, that is where the boxiest cuts end up.

Spot 5: Build a relationship with regular dealers. A seller who knows your size and taste will set a piece aside before it ever reaches the rack.
