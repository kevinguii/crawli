```
A vintage tee and a modern reprint can look identical in a listing photo, but five physical details give the fake away every time.

Tell 1: Read the tag first. Tag fonts, union labels, and care symbols date a shirt fast. A modern RN number or a country of origin that did not exist in the era is an instant tell.
Tell 2: Look for single-stitch hems. Most tees before the late 90s used single-stitch sleeve and bottom hems. Double-stitch usually means it was made later, no matter what the seller claims.
Tell 3: Know the blank brands. Screen Stars, Hanes Fifty-Fifty, Tultex, and Anvil were the era workhorses. Learning the common blanks dates the shirt before you even see the graphic.
Tell 4: Read the fade and feel. Real age gives a soft, broken-in hand and even sun-fading. Artificial distressing looks blotchy and the fabric still feels stiff underneath.
Tell 5: Inspect the print. Vintage plastisol prints crack and soften with age. A perfectly crisp, rubbery print on a "70s" tee is a reproduction nine times out of ten.

Once you can read these, vintage tee shopping gets a lot less risky and a lot more fun.

```

**TikTok Title**

5 ways to tell real leather from a convincing fake
```

**TikTok Description**
```
Real leather ages beautifully and can last decades. Bonded or faux leather peels and cracks within a year. Here are five ways to tell them apart before you buy.

Test 1: Check the label. Look for "genuine" or "full-grain leather" on the tag. Vague terms like "man-made material" or "leather-look" are a polite way of saying plastic.
Test 2: Feel the texture. Real leather is soft, supple, and warms to your touch. Synthetic feels cold, uniform, and a little plasticky no matter how good the print is.
Test 3: Look for natural grain. Genuine hide has irregular pores and tiny imperfections. A pattern that repeats perfectly across the whole surface is printed, not grown.
Test 4: Try the water test. A small drop of water absorbs slowly into real leather and darkens it briefly. On synthetic it just beads up and sits on the surface.
Test 5: Trust reputable makers. Brands with a leather heritage stake their name on it. When in doubt, a known maker and an honest seller beat a too-good price every time.

Knowing your materials is the difference between an heirloom and a letdown.
