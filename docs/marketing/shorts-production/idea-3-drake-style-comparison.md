# Idea #3: Drake-Style Comparison (No vs. Yes)

**Format:** Two-panel vertical layout showing rejection (top) and approval (bottom). You in two poses.  
**Runtime:** 3 seconds  
**Audience hook:** Reframes the choice from "guessing" (risky) to "checking" (data-driven). Language-file §1: this audience thinks in "comps" and "evidence," not vibes.  
**Why it wins:** Meme template people already know. Founder-led = authentic. The reframe is profile-aligned.

## Shooting script

**Setup:** Camera on you, neutral background. You'll shoot two separate takes (reject pose, approve pose), then combine them in Resolve into top/bottom panels.

**Take 1: The rejection pose**
- Position: Sit or stand in the middle of frame, neutral starting position.
- Action: Hand out in front of you (palm down), shake your head left-to-right slowly. Frown or neutral-to-negative expression.
- Vibe: "Absolutely not. Bad idea."
- Hold: 2–3 seconds.
- Close: Let your hand drop naturally.

**Take 2: The approval pose**
- Position: Same starting position (so you can cut between them easily in Resolve).
- Action: Thumbs up with a smile, OR hand to heart with a nod, OR just a slow confident nod with a small satisfied smile. Pick ONE gesture and commit to it.
- Vibe: "Yeah. This is the way."
- Hold: 2–3 seconds.
- Close: Hold the smile/gesture through the fade.

**Timing:** Each take = 2–3 seconds.

**Shoot 2–3 takes of each** so you have options.

**Tip:** Shoot in the same lighting, same position, same distance from camera. This makes the top/bottom edit feel seamless.

## DaVinci Resolve assembly

1. **Canvas split:** Use the Fusion page or the Edit page with video track positioning.
   - Import your reject clip and approve clip as separate video tracks.
   - Apply Transform on each:
     - **Top panel:** Crop/scale the reject clip to fit the top half of the 1080×1350 canvas. Position it at Y=0.
     - **Bottom panel:** Crop/scale the approve clip to fit the bottom half. Position it at Y=675 (halfway down a 1350px-tall frame).
   - Alternative: Use two separate media pools, drag them into Fusion as text+ media, arrange them top/bottom.

2. **Timeline:**
   - 0:00–1:30: Reject clip + text "Guessing the price and hoping it sells" fades in at 0:00, stays until 1:30
   - 1:30–3:00: Approve clip appears, text "Checking what it actually sold for first" fades in at 1:30, stays until 3:00
   - 3:00+: Both fade to black or to a still shot of Crawli (optional)

3. **Text overlays:**
   - Reject text: "Guessing the price and hoping it sells" — bold, white, 18pt, top-center or top-left (keep out of top 20%)
   - Approve text: "Checking what it actually sold for first" — bold, white, 18pt, bottom-center or bottom-left (keep out of bottom 20%)
   - Use the same font, size, and drop shadow for both so they feel cohesive

4. **Audio:**
   - Optional "nope" sound at 0:00 (1 sec duration)
   - Optional approval sound at 1:30 ("ding," "yes," or uplifting music cue)
   - Or just silence — the contrast reads visually

5. **Color:** White text with dark drop shadow for readability on any background. Test in both light/dark mode.

6. **Export:** 1080×1350 (4:5), 30fps.

## Why this works for Shorts

1. **Meme template:** Audiences already know the format (Drake-style comparison). Zero explanation needed.
2. **Founder-led:** You on camera beats faceless product shots. Shorts rewards personality.
3. **Reframe, not claim:** You're not claiming Crawli is magic. You're showing a choice (guess vs. check) and picking the smarter one. Profile-aligned.
4. **Gesture language:** The visual contrast (hand pushing away vs. thumbs up) is clearer than words. Universal.
5. **Fast edit:** 3 seconds is snappy. Shorts algorithm loves it.

---

## Production notes

- **Gesture clarity:** Whatever you choose (thumbs up, hand on heart, nod), make sure it's *clearly* readable on a phone screen. Big, obvious movements.
- **Matching lighting:** Since you're splitting these two clips, shooting them back-to-back in the same lighting/position makes the final edit feel like one cohesive piece.
- **Text consistency:** Same font, size, and drop shadow for both lines. It reads as intentional, not thrown together.
- **No voice:** This format doesn't need voiceover. Sound design (optional ding/buzzer) or silence both work.
