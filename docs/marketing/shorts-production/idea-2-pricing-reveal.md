# Idea #2: Split-Screen Pricing Reveal

**Format:** Left = asking price (static or screenshot), Right = sold comps from Crawli (appears on reveal)  
**Runtime:** 3–4 seconds  
**Audience hook:** Idea E from idea-triage.md — "asking prices are not comps... sold comps expose the truth" (language-file §1, unprompted from the Reddit corpus)  
**Why it wins:** The numbers do the talking. Your reaction validates the emotion. Zero claims needed — it's just data.

## Shooting script for on-camera moment

**Setup:** Camera on you, neutral background. You're sitting at a desk or standing in front of a clean wall. Lighting: natural window light.

**Take 1: The "shock" reaction**
- Open: You're looking at the imaginary screen (a point on the wall behind the camera, or slightly to the side). Neutral expression.
- Cue: As the screen recording shows the lower sold price, make a surprised face.
- Action: Eyes widen, eyebrows go up, maybe a slight head tilt (like "wait, what?"). If it feels natural, touch your chest or put your hand to your mouth (genuine surprise gesture).
- Hold: 2–3 seconds. Let the shock register.
- Close: Small smile or exhale (like "wow, I almost made a mistake").

**Timing:** 2–3 seconds of clean footage.

**Shoot 2–3 takes** so you have options.

## DaVinci Resolve assembly

1. **Canvas setup:** Use a split-screen effect (two video tracks, cropped and positioned left/right, or use the Fusion page to create two rectangles side-by-side).
   - Left side: Static screenshot of the asking-price listing (just an image, no video)
   - Right side: Screen recording of Crawli showing sold comps
   
2. **Timeline:**
   - 0:00–1:00: Left screenshot + text "$180" fades in
   - 1:00–1:50: Right screen recording starts playing (Crawli results). Add a "whoosh" sound at 1:00. Text "$95" or "Actually sold for $95" fades in on the right.
   - 1:50–2:30: Your reaction shot (on camera)
   - All text fades out at 2:30

3. **Text overlays:**
   - "$180" (left, 40pt, bold, white with drop shadow)
   - "$95" or "Actually sold for $95" (right, 40pt, bold, white with drop shadow)
   - (Optional: a callout like "Gap: $85" in a neutral color, centered, appears at 1:50)

4. **Audio:**
   - Quiet waiting tone (0:00–1:00)
   - Whoosh/reveal sound (1:00–1:10)
   - Silence or subtle background (1:10–end)

5. **Color:** Make sure the prices are visible in both light and dark mode. Use white text with a dark drop shadow for contrast.

6. **Export:** 1080×1350 (4:5), 30fps.

## Asset list

### Screenshots you need to capture:
- **Asking-price listing:** Find a real item on Poshmark or Grailed that you're actually interested in (or just use a realistic recent example from your own sourcing). Screenshot the title + price. Crop to show just the item and price clearly. The price should be the focal point.
  
- **Crawli sold comps:** Log into Crawli, search for the same item (or a very similar one), navigate to the sold comps section. Screenshot the completed-sale prices. Make sure at least 2–3 sales are visible, all showing lower prices than the asking price. This should be REAL data, not faked.

### Screen recording (Crawli):
- Record yourself doing the search in Crawli, showing the transition from "searching" to "sold comps appear." Alternatively, just record the static sold comps screen for 1 second and use that.

### Sounds (royalty-free):
- **Reveal sound** (1–2 seconds): "Card flip," "page turn," or "whoosh"
  - YouTube Audio Library: search "reveal" or "surprise"
  - Freesound.org: search "card flip" or "notification reveal"
  
- **Shock reaction sound** (optional): You can just use silence, or add a subtle "ding" or "bell" sound when your reaction appears.

### Reaction shot:
- Just you on camera (filmed earlier) showing genuine surprise.

## Why this works for Shorts

1. **Pure data contrast:** The gap between $180 and $95 is the hook. No explanation needed.
2. **Validation face:** Your shock reaction makes it credible. If you're surprised, the viewer is too.
3. **Idea-triage alignment:** This is literally Idea E, hook #1 from the approved hooks list. It's already been scored as top-tier by the audience research.
4. **C1 compliant:** This only shows Grailed or Poshmark completed sales, so the "actually sold for" claim is factual (not a blended price). Zero risk.
5. **Short and sharp:** 3–4 seconds is perfect for Shorts' algorithm, which favors quick hooks.

---

## Production notes

- **Real data only:** Use an actual item you're researching, or something currently on the marketplace. Faked numbers tank credibility with this audience (profile.md: they distrust hype language).
- **Platform consistency:** If showing Poshmark on the left, show Poshmark/Grailed/Vinted sold data on the right. Don't mix Mercari sold data with Poshmark asking prices (C1 forbids it, and it reads as made-up).
- **Bold the numbers:** Make sure the prices are the visual focal point. Small text = less impact.
